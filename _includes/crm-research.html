Evidence behind these findings

Material mix. At a given dependence rank, a one-standard-deviation higher material-price-risk basket is associated with 0.74 percentage points higher annualized stock volatility and 0.063 points higher expected shortfall. The price-risk association with stock volatility strengthens by 2.19 points from the bottom to the top of the dependence ranking. The paper uses exposure-robust uncertainty for these estimates.

Material connections. A one-standard-deviation higher material-connection rank is associated with 0.041 correlation points more weekly market-adjusted return comovement, and 0.049 among pairs in different three-digit industries. One correlation point is 0.01 in raw correlation. These are incremental associations; they do not establish a return premium or the share of portfolio risk caused by materials.

Sources, units, and interpretation

Measurement · From physical requirements to cost exposure. Technical evidence establishes material-specific production links; input cost shares turn those links into product cost elasticities; product revenues aggregate them to firm dependence. The network drawing is schematic. In the cost-validation regressions, predicted cost pressure is measured relative to identified revenue, while realized changes in cost of goods sold are scaled by initial sales. Their estimated magnitudes closely match; the comparison does not equate these two denominators. Source: Dollar Responses per Percentage Point of Predicted Cost Pressure.

A · How firms adjust. We trace cash flow and financing after material-price changes, comparing the component associated with identified supply-tightening events with the remaining price variation. For a predicted cost increase of 1% of identified revenue, the event-driven estimates at quarter 4 imply cumulative cash flow lower by 0.391% of initial assets and net external financing higher by 0.692%. This pattern is consistent with firms meeting liquidity needs through external finance. Shading and error bars show 95% confidence intervals; “Before” shows pre-event responses. The comparison series labeled “Predictable price changes” also includes pre-announcement price movements.

B · What past events imply for firms today. We apply historical event-driven price changes to firms’ 2024–2025 material exposures, using the estimated quarter-4 cash-flow and financing responses. Across all 27 priced-event replays, the implied amounts are $20.0 billion of cash flow at risk and $35.4 billion of external financing. These are scenarios, not recorded losses; totals include offsetting price declines. The brown segments show relationships where specialist reports miss uses accounting for more than half of a firm’s material dependence. Their size shows how relying on those reports alone can miss financially important exposure.

C · Individual and shared equity risk. The material-mix results compare users with positive dependence on priced materials, controlling for dependence, firm characteristics, country-by-year and six-digit industry-by-year effects. Material-price risk is the dependence-weighted volatility of the firm’s materials; downside risk is expected shortfall. The basket and interaction estimates use exposure-robust standard errors. The return-comovement test uses quarterly Fama–MacBeth regressions over 2019–2025, controlling for industry, products, country, firm characteristics and past return correlation, with Newey–West standard errors. Material connection weights the covariance of material prices measured before the quarter by the two firms’ dependence. Both the full-sample and different-industry coefficients are significant at the 5% level. Sources: Material Price Risk and Market Outcomes, Panels A–B, and Material Connection and Return Comovement, columns 3–4. The results identify associations; they do not isolate material prices as the cause of observed return comovement.

Where the exposure sits and what is visible. About half of the variation in dependence ranks lies within six-digit industry–year groups. In 2019, 3,165 of 8,073 firms (39.2%) have positive measured dependence on at least one material. For 25 of the 55 materials, a single product blocks every mapped route to downstream products that together account for at least 90% of measured dependence. Documented relationships carry 38.3% of measured dependence in firms’ filings and 41.0% in specialist reports, each in its matched sample. Announcement returns are three-day returns around 27 supply events, 2017–2025; firms that mine or refine raw materials earn about 1.1 percentage points more than firms without measured dependence.

A technological source of financing needs and equity risk, recognized only where it is most salient.
